01 — About

Peace, in Catalan

A short account of how I ended up chasing a discrepancy in the eighth decimal place.

Liverpool, UK · 53.4°N

My name is Pau, which means peace in Catalan. I grew up in Cardedeu, a small town near Barcelona, and left it for Theoretical Physics at the University of Manchester. The subject took hold, and I now do a PhD at Liverpool on one of the sharpest open cracks in the Standard Model: the muon g−2 anomaly.

Day to day that means two things at once. I evaluate Feynman integrals, the objects that encode how particles talk to one another, by integrating their differential equations numerically. And I make those evaluations fast enough to live inside a Monte Carlo event generator, so that a theory prediction can actually be compared against what a detector saw.

Along the way I picked up the Tessella Prize for best use of software in a master's thesis, a First Class degree, and, in a previous life, a gold medal at the Catalan National League of Debate. My PhD is funded by the Leverhulme Trust.

Away from the terminal I dance salsa and bachata, eat, run, and build small things for the pleasure of it. A few of them are further down this page.

Books

The Kingkiller Chronicle · Rothfuss

This has to be first on the list. Rothfuss' books are a masterpiece of fantasy, which I devoured when I was younger. Rereading them now doesn't make them any less good — maybe nostalgia is altering my judgement, but who cares? Very unfortunate that we will never know how they end.

21 Lessons for the 21st Century · Harari

My first book by Harari — yes, before Sapiens — and I understand why he is so popular. It touches on many topics, few of them light, yet reads easily and gives plenty of food for thought. I had to stop more than once to argue with myself about the ideas in it.

Read more

Fictions · Borges

I do not like the writing style of this book — I am one of those who believes writing can be both simple and beautiful. Nevertheless it is one of the best books I have ever read. Almost every story is, by itself, one great idea. I can't vouch for the English translation, but the original is a masterpiece.

Read more

The Three-Body Problem · Liu

I don't really understand the excellent reviews. The initial world is genuinely interesting, but the book commits the worst crime a sci-fi book can commit, in my opinion: it sets up rules and then doesn't respect them. I can believe in aliens with a different understanding of physics; I can't understand why sophons that can alter vision and sabotage large-scale experiments can't simply kill the main characters. Maybe I should read the rest of the trilogy. I don't think I will.
